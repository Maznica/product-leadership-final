# Product Strategy & OKRs: Fable Growth

> Module 1 · Write a Strategy That Can Survive a Test

## 1. Playing to Win cascade

| Question | Your choice |
|---|---|
| **Winning aspiration** | Winning means Fable’s users don’t just get through a hard stretch: they stay well afterwards, turning the calm they found into a light daily practice that keeps them steady and ready for the next one. For Fable, that means becoming the default “keeping well” habit for adults aged 25–45. We’ll measure it by how many users keep a weekly practice routine beyond day 90, and by the premium renewals that follow. |
| **Where to play** | Fable will play for its premium “stress graduates”: adults aged 25–45 who used Fable intensively to get through a hard stretch and, between days 60 and 120, are ready to move from recovering to staying well. We focus on keeping-well use cases (daily routines, early-warning check-ins before a relapse and a return path when life gets hard again), built on the sleep and meditation core. We deliberately leave out clinical and crisis care, the broad “curious beginner” market owned by Headspace and Calm, and new age groups, countries and employer sales for now. |
| **How to win** | Fable wins by owning the moment no competitor serves: the day a user feels better. Its “Fable Steady” journey turns each user’s own recovery history into a personal keeping-well ritual, with a graduation moment, short non-clinical skills, monthly check-ins and a one-tap return when life gets hard again. That combination is hard to copy, because it rests on personal history competitors don’t have, trust earned at the user’s hardest moment, and a focus that Headspace and Calm (moving into clinical care and employers) and AI chatbots (facing regulation) would have to turn away from their own direction to match. |
| **Capabilities required** | To deliver Fable Steady, the team must be excellent at three things it owns: personalizing a keeping-well ritual from each user’s own recovery history, designing habits that turn feeling better into a light daily routine, and creating short non-clinical skills content with the clinical advisors. Safety and compliance, privacy, lifecycle messaging and experimentation tools are must-haves we partner for or buy, so the team of 14 is organized into two mission squads plus a “Steady Studio” for content. The biggest gaps to close are data and personalization, and safety and regulatory governance. |
| **Management systems** | Each quarter, we judge the strategy by one North Star (users past day 90 who keep their weekly practice routine) and its drivers: graduation conversion, routine completion, day-120 and day-180 retention by cohort, return rate and premium renewal among graduates, with wellbeing and notification opt-outs as guardrails. Squads review the metrics weekly, leadership looks monthly at whether each new cohort retains better, a monthly safety review keeps Fable non-clinical, and a quarterly board review scores the OKRs and tests the riskiest assumptions. The first 90 days run four experiments with ship, iterate and kill thresholds agreed in advance, and a pre-agreed rule triggers a change of course if graduates don’t take up Steady within two quarters. |

## 2. Your one hard no

> **Hard no:** We will not build an open community or social feed where users share their mental-health struggles, even though it could create strong network effects. It would pull our small team into moderation, safety work and the slow job of getting a community going, and it would keep users tied to the identity of “someone going through something,” the opposite of the graduation that Fable Steady is built on. Saying no keeps the team of 14 focused on one thing: making each user’s own keeping-well ritual so personal that they stay once they feel better. Social stays limited to sharing practice, never problems.

## 3. OKR cascade

> **Objective:** Make Fable people’s long-term companion for staying well, not just a rescue in high-stress moments.
>
> - **KR1:** 35% of users who reach day 60 start a personal Steady Plan (from 0% today, because the plan doesn’t exist yet)
> - **KR2:** The share of users past day 90 who keep a weekly practice routine (3 or more sessions a week) rises from an estimated ~10% to 20%.
> - **KR3:** Day-120 retention of users who joined during a stressful period rises from an estimated ~20% to 30%.

> - **Revenue check (tracked with the KRs):** Premium renewal among subscribers past day 90 rises from an estimated ~40% to 50%.
## 4. AI pressure-test

**Most valid challenge:** The most valid challenge is the assumption that stress graduates want an ongoing practice once they feel fine, because the whole strategy rests on it. If recovery is naturally episodic, a daily ritual won’t hold users and retention won’t improve. That’s why we test it first, through experiment E1, before building the full initiative.

**What I will change and what I will defend:** Change: I’ll run E1 before committing the full budget. If the assumption holds, I’ll replace KR1 (“start a Steady Plan”), which measures adoption of our own feature and can be inflated by automatic sign-ups, with “the share of users reaching day 60 who are still practicing with their plan four weeks later.” I’ll also add a revenue check on premium renewal among subscribers past day 90, because the board funded this to keep paying users. Defend: I’ll keep the focus on stress graduates and the hard no on an open community, because that’s where the churn and the premium revenue are, and it keeps a team of 14 focused. Employer sales stay a pre-agreed fallback if E1 fails, not a day-one bet, because a sales channel would split the team before the core habit is proven.
